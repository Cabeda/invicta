Walk through the awkward cases

What this experiment can settle

The access rule can be expressed in SUMO without giving every private car a resident exemption. Ordinary crossing trips remain possible, and through trips are rerouted or explicitly recorded as having no permitted route. The bus result is sensitive to assumed traffic and permits; it is not yet evidence for a real-world reliability target.

Full relevant model state and limitations

The imported baseline had stretches with two general lanes plus a generated contraflow lane. Eight directed stretches were normalized to one lane per mapped direction for this two-lane experiment. Junction geometry and signal timings are generated, with import warnings retained. No field survey, calibrated traffic counts, observed dwell, resident registry, pedestrian demand, freight, parking-search behaviour, mode shift or network-wide route equilibrium is included. Rerouting occurs within the buffered district; routes outside it are not represented.

Timetable stop calls define intended arrivals. Each stop uses an assumed 20-second dwell and holds against its departure time. Buses start at their first mapped corridor stop. Entry queues appear as insertion delay; upstream observed bus lateness is not supplied. The displayed punctuality measure is arrivals no more than three minutes late at the final mapped stop, with unfinished bus stop sequences counted as failures. It is not an STCP performance standard or a full-route measure.

Potential rollout and costs
  1. Establish the baseline: survey the lane layout and permitted turns; obtain matching traffic, AVL, boarding/dwell and controller data; define resident eligibility and essential service access.
  2. Design a reversible access trial: check junction movements and diversion routes; locate signs/enforcement points and loading alternatives; establish permit administration and monitoring.
  3. Measure the trial: compare bus reliability, local access, crossing queues, displaced traffic and compliance against the baseline. Recalibrate before estimating permanent benefits.
  4. Consider permanent works: only where measured results justify them; price markings, signs, enforcement, stop/access works, design and recurring operations from scoped local evidence.

No euro amounts or delivery durations are invented here. They require a bill of quantities, feasibility decisions and price sources.
