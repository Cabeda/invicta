# European bus reliability practices relevant to the 701

Research checked on 30 October 2026. These selected examples support investigating targeted queue bypass, conditional signal priority, kerb management and operating recovery. They establish neither a street design nor a quantified Porto benefit.

## 1. Zürich: protect a short congested section, including its approaches

The city describes the Zürich approach as a combination of reserved alignments, signal priority, control-centre operations and traffic restrictions. Its Hohlstrasse example is a reversible bus lane, opened in October 2018, between Hardplatz and Herdernstrasse. A council response dated **1 March 2023**, **GR 2022/601, decision 437/2023, p. 4**, reports that evening-peak median extra running time relative to undisturbed operation fell from **37 seconds in 2017 to 12 seconds after implementation**. This is a city-reported field before/after result; the paragraph omits the post-intervention sampling year, sample size and controlled counterfactual. It cannot isolate contributing changes or predict Porto savings. [City council response](https://www.gemeinderat-zuerich.ch/dokumente/641bcf5aab90472c80201db4d524c7cd-332?filename=2022_0601#page=4).

The **21 March 2018** project announcement explains the physical trade: reserve the centre of three lanes for buses in either direction, relocate one bus stop beyond a junction, rebuild stops for accessibility and add cycle lanes. This was a street-and-stop project, not just a controller setting. [City project announcement](https://www.stadt-zuerich.ch/misc/de/mitteilungsarchiv/medienmitteilungen/2018/03/180321a3.html).

**701 implication:** investigate short protected approaches. A reversible centre lane requires suitable width, bus movements, stops and crossing arrangements; this is not a Costa Cabral design recommendation.

## 2. Eindhoven: give different priority to late, on-time and early buses

Eindhoven's municipal explanation gives a particularly clear operational rule. A late bus, or an HOV bus, receives priority as soon as safe margins allow; other traffic is stopped. For a non-HOV bus running on time, the currently green traffic finishes its green and the bus receives the next green. A non-HOV bus running early receives no priority. The same page explains pedestrian clearance time: conflicting traffic remains red so someone already crossing can finish safely. The page is **undated; accessed 30 October 2026**. This verifies a published operating policy, not a measured journey-time outcome. [Municipality: safe and smooth travel, sections on bus priority and pedestrian clearance](https://www.eindhoven.nl/stad-en-wonen/verkeer/veilig-en-vlot-door-de-stad).

Dutch CROW guidance, published **11 April 2022**, explains that helping an early bus can increase early running, while repeated interventions can excessively disrupt other traffic. This supports conditional priority; it is design guidance, not a trial or a uniform Dutch operating rule. [CROW guidance](https://kennisbank.crow.nl/public/gastgebruiker/VMGT/Handboek_verkeerslichtenregelingen_2022/Geconditioneerde_prioriteit_voor_het_openbaar_vervoer/114499).

**701 implication:** help journeys that need it, retaining pedestrian clearance, service to cross traffic and downstream space. The bus must reach the stop line.

## 3. London: coordinate queue bypass and signals, with street access designed around them

TfL's **November 2025** guidance describes differential signal priority for late buses and pre-signals that hold general traffic while a bus leaves its lane. Existing examples include Stockwell Road approaching Clapham Road and Dog Kennel Hill. The bypass must be long enough to pass the queue; coordination with downstream signals can keep cars out of the space ahead. This is documented practice/design guidance, without a measured benefit for these examples. **[TfL guidance, pp. 27-28](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/bus-priority-best-practice-design-guidance-nov-2025.pdf#page=27).**

The same guidance identifies the constraints: queues can mask moving buses from pedestrians; access restrictions can affect the wider network; loading should be reviewed with businesses and relocated or controlled where necessary; compliance and enforcement determine whether a measure works. **[TfL guidance, pp. 24, 26, 28 and 40](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/bus-priority-best-practice-design-guidance-nov-2025.pdf).**

**701 implication:** test whether bypass and junction clearance displace obstruction to the next junction, crossing or delivery bay.

## 4. London: manage the service as well as the road

TfL's **November 2024** planning guidelines require running times based on current traffic and passenger conditions, and recovery time at route ends. Recovery requires a stand, safe forward turning and nearby driver welfare facilities. The guidelines also warn that adding time on long routes can produce early running and onboard waiting to restore spacing, slowing passenger journeys. These are planning requirements and tradeoffs, not an experimental estimate of recovery-time benefits. **[TfL planning guidelines, pp. 9 and 20](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/bus-service-planning-guidelines.pdf#page=9).**

TfL's route-report explanation distinguishes frequent service (**five or more buses/hour**), where even intervals and excess waiting time matter, from less frequent service, where passengers rely more on the timetable. iBus records arrivals and departures and lets controllers detect bunching and regulate the service. The webpage is **undated; accessed 30 October 2026**. [TfL route reports: common questions](https://tfl.gov.uk/forms/14144.aspx?borough=Barking).

**701 implication:** evaluate timetable adherence and passenger waiting separately; match regulation to frequency. London’s thresholds are not automatically STCP acceptance criteria.

## 5. Ghent: preserve destination access while removing through-car routes

Ghent's 2017 circulation plan divides the centre into six sectors; car travel between sectors uses the inner ring, while selected modes and permitted users can pass restrictions. This preserves ways to reach destinations without keeping every street available as a car shortcut. It is an access/circulation model, not a rule that all resident cars may cross every filter. [City explanation](https://stad.gent/en/mobility-ghent/circulation-plan).

The city's **11 June 2019** evaluation reported sustained changes toward cycling and public transport, but also higher traffic on some axes, some worse car journeys, and respondents shopping less often. Those observations are not a controlled causal estimate of retail turnover. [City evaluation](https://persruimte.stad.gent/173408-evaluatie-circulatieplan-gentenaars-kiezen-vaker-voor-fiets-en-openbaar-vervoer/).

**701 implication:** give residents, shoppers and deliveries explicit access routes and measure displaced traffic. A general citywide mode-shift assumption is not a substitute for those journeys.

## Evidence boundaries and implications for Porto

London is not a solved-reliability example. TfL's current account of **Q1 2026/27, 1 April-26 June 2026**, reports continuing reliability pressure since 2022/23 and declining bus speeds. Its practices demonstrate tools and institutional responsibilities; they do not prove that those tools overcome every source of congestion or disruption. [TfL performance data and commentary](https://tfl.gov.uk/corporate/publications-and-reports/buses-performance-data).

The proposed transfer is a design hypothesis: combine usable priority through a specific bottleneck, safe and conditional signal control, a workable plan for local access/loading, enforcement, and realistic recovery. Compare completed service, checkpoint performance, headway variation and passenger waiting, while also checking cross traffic, pedestrian waits and displaced queues. No percentage or number of seconds above is a forecast for Porto.

The Hohlstrasse number is the only quantified intervention outcome used here. Eindhoven and the London design/operations sources establish published practice, not measured local causal effects. No cited result is a SUMO result, and none validates the current 701 model or classifies its unresolved failures. The existing [701 investigation](701-investigation-results-2026-10-30.md) remains the relevant source for those model limitations.

## Recommendation for the current 701 model

This is our proposed test programme, inferred from the European examples and the recorded Invicta failures. It is not a selected street design or a claim that a package meets the timetable.

### A focused package to test

1. **Pires de Lima–Combatentes first.** Test clear bus approaches, stopping/loading management, a short queue bypass or upstream pre-signal where the geometry permits it, and coordinated priority at the actual affected junctions. Size an approach to the observed queue, rather than assuming any short strip of bus lane is enough. Preserve crossings and accessible boarding. Other bus routes sharing the junction need to be represented when data permits.
2. **Conditional priority.** Compare the existing proximity-based controller with a controller that uses schedule deviation, checks downstream space, resolves opposing bus requests, and records grants/denials and effects on other users. Early buses should not receive recovery priority; no early departures remain an acceptance requirement. Thresholds and maximum requests are explicit experiment settings, not copied Portuguese operating standards.
3. **Local access and shop servicing.** Keep property and business access, disabled drop-offs, deliveries and emergency routes. Test short access sections and crossing-only junctions against a broader resident-permit design. If a point filter is needed, give each destination a mapped access route; a resident exemption should not automatically make the corridor an unrestricted through route. Model taxi/motorcycle/resident exemptions as actual traffic. Loading locations and permitted times must be compatible with clear approaches.
4. **Alfena as a separate problem.** Retain the existing-layout comparison and isolate which proposed changes around Rua de São Vicente interact with its persistent queue. Do not select broader restrictions or disable junction keep-clear rules based on the final snapshot. Keep the current candidate rejected until the initiating mechanism is understood and the remedy tested.
5. **Whole-line operations.** Link services into explicit feasible vehicle circulations, including short workings, turnaround/recovery, driver constraints and finite spares. Public data has not established STCP's actual vehicle blocks, so inferred blocks must be labelled and varied. Add crowding, boarding and accessibility cases; a faster boarding setting needs an operational interpretation.

The first two implementation seams are concrete. [signal_priority.py](../../prototypes/line-701/full/signal_priority.py) currently uses proximity, colour and speed for requests without consulting timetable lateness or downstream queue storage. [priority_search.py](../../prototypes/line-701/full/priority_search.py) emits a separate native vehicle for every scheduled service; late arrival of one service does not currently delay the same bus's following service. Origin punctuality in that model cannot validate fleet recovery. These are code observations, not claims about STCP's operations.

### A bounded route to a credible result

- **Resolve model questions before claiming success.** Capture the actual onset of the Alfena and Costa Cabral queues from original inputs, inspect connector conflicts, signal phases, leaders and downstream occupancy, and give the three stop-position flags and empty-case 19-second miss explicit evidence-backed dispositions. An imported control plan is not a verified municipal plan. The failed snapshot continuation and reduced two-car reproducer cannot replace an original onset trace.
- **Use local diagnoses for discovery; retain whole-line acceptance.** Build small regression cases only after their boundary behaviour is checked against the original run. Compare no change, conditional signals, an approach/kerb intervention, and their interaction. Keep ordinary-vehicle endpoints, passenger requests and the official reference fixed. This avoids repeatedly paying for a full regional run before a local hypothesis has evidence.
- **Test the complete service, then robustness.** A promising package must still run all 127 published services in both directions, including short workings, with no disappearing demand. Retain the 714 strict timing checks; report operator-contract indicators and passenger waiting/headway measures separately. Use paired discovery seeds and a held-out uncertainty/verification matrix as described in the [earlier methods research](bus-reliability-best-practices-2026-10-30.md).
- **Find a smaller successful tested package.** Only after a package passes, remove one measure at a time and recheck. Compare reserved lane length, affected junctions, stop works and fleet resources, alongside costs and access impacts. This establishes necessity within the tested catalogue, not a mathematical global minimum.
- **Separate opening-day and adoption scenarios.** First test unchanged mode choices and displaced car routes. Then test explicit adoption assumptions, preserving travellers and accounting for bus capacity, access/walk time and people who continue driving. Do not make congestion disappear by deleting trips or assume that every individual benefits.
- **Pilot with monitoring.** After a credible design and the relevant approvals, stage reversible signs/markings, controller settings, enforcement and servicing changes, then permanent works if results justify them. Show bus punctuality and passenger-time distributions, resident and non-resident travel/access effects, side-street queues, delivery impacts and pedestrian waits. Do not buy apparent bus success by leaving other requests unfinished.

Our strict objective remains all required scheduled checks passing under the declared conditions. Neither European examples nor finite simulated success justify promising that every real bus will always arrive at an exact second. If the verified vehicle/boarding/geometry assumptions make a published interval infeasible, identify the needed operating resource, physical change or explicit timetable decision; do not silently relax the reference.
