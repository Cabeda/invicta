# Bus reliability: best practices and the next 701 experiments

Researched 30 October 2026. Scope: public primary sources, established operating guidance and selected original research. This is a recommendation for improving the investigation; it does not establish a timetable-compliant 701 proposal.

The strongest recommendation is to **diagnose and verify the model, target individual sources of delay, then test a small combination of operating and street changes**. Adding more bus lanes or a more sophisticated optimiser to an unresolved model is unlikely to make the findings more credible. FHWA explicitly puts error checking before calibration and alternatives analysis; TCRP's reliability guide treats diagnosis, measurement and intervention as one continuing programme. [FHWA error checking](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop18036/chapter4.htm), [TCRP 215, *Minutes Matter* (2020)](https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/25727).

## What the primary sources support

| Approach | Evidence and limits | Application to Invicta |
|---|---|---|
| Verify geometry and vehicle behaviour before optimisation | FHWA recommends reviewing network, demand, controls and vehicle defaults, then tracing individual vehicles at very low demand to find unexpected slowing. This is established modelling guidance, not proof that any particular Porto input is wrong. [FHWA chapter 4](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop18036/chapter4.htm) | The new ModelAudit and short reproduction path are the right first step. Inspect the first failed interval and the heads of queues. |
| Calibrate bottleneck dynamics and corridor performance | Defaults are not calibration. FHWA recommends representative observed days, variation envelopes and local/system measures; unstable or unrealistic gridlocked runs should be investigated before alternatives analysis. [FHWA calibration](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop18036/chapter5.htm) | Do not tune `sigma`, acceleration, boarding or demand until the timetable passes. | Coordinate conditional priority and bus control | Original analytical/simulation work finds that requesting signal priority when it helps reliability can outperform unconditional priority while issuing fewer requests. The findings cover scheduled and headway-controlled services under the paper's assumptions. [Anderson and Daganzo, published 2020; authors' institution](https://its.berkeley.edu/node/4575) | Compare late-bus priority plus early-bus holding against the present controller on identical inputs. |

[FHWA alternatives analysis](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop18036/chapter6.htm) | Use paired seeds and fixed physical demand; separate exploratory seeds from final verification seeds. Three successful seeds would not establish universal reliability. |

## Preserve the target, but make its meaning explicit

The user's acceptance target remains all 127 published workings, both directions, no missing trips, exact origin and designated intermediate departures, and terminal arrivals no later than scheduled. Do not silently replace it with a tolerance window or a headway objective.

There are three different questions:

1. **Declared simulation compliance:** did every required checkpoint pass in this execution?
2. **Robustness:** does the unchanged proposal pass across the declared demand, disturbance, seed and uncertainty cases?
3. **Field reliability:** do observed services meet the operator's agreed operational measure?

Operational practice uses explicit windows and probabilities. For example, England's published statistical guidance defines a non-frequent on-time window of one minute early to 5:59 late and measures frequent service using excess waiting time; failed services must remain in the denominator. That is an illustration of measurement design, not a proposed replacement for this project's goal or evidence about Portugal. This initial survey did not establish an STCP contractual window; the subsequent [STCP definition audit](701-punctuality-definition-2026-10-30.md) identifies the signed contract's different events, windows and denominators. Neither indicator replaces the strict project target. [DfT measurement guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f983abc8fa8f543f3e91424/bus-punctuality-questionnaire-guidance-notes.pdf).

A timetable value represented as `05:33:00` also needs source semantics: an exact departure timing point differs from an interpolated intermediate reference. GTFS explicitly distinguishes exact and approximate times through `timepoint`. The repo already separates designated checks; preserve that distinction and record the operator API's actual precision instead of treating a seconds field alone as an operational guarantee. [GTFS Schedule reference, `stop_times.txt`](https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/reference/#stop_timestxt).

Zero failures in a finite collection of stochastic runs cannot prove a zero real-world failure probability. As a mathematical illustration, with independent identical Bernoulli *whole-day* trials and zero failures, the one-sided 95% upper failure bound is `1 − 0.05^(1/n)`. The independence and identical-distribution assumptions do not hold automatically for trips/checkpoints on the same day; 714 correlated checks must not be counted as 714 independent experiments. [NIST exact binomial confidence limits](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbici.htm).

## What this changes in our model review

The current evidence still contains a 19-second miss in the empty diagnostic and a full-traffic package with 85 completed workings versus its baseline's 119. Those findings require explanation before selecting a successful package. They do not establish that the real timetable is impossible. [Current study and frozen evidence](../../prototypes/line-701/full/PRIORITY_SEARCH.md).

The accompanying bounded control reproduced all 25 completed native bus calls before its cutoff, including the first Ermesinde departure at 05:33:19. A separately labelled `bus.sigma=0` sensitivity departed at 05:33:05: a 14-second improvement, still a failure. These are local diagnostic results, not full-service or field qualification. SUMO documents `sigma` and other stochastic mechanisms as model behaviour, with reproducibility dependent on inputs, seeds and version. [SUMO randomness](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Randomness.html).

Recommended review rules:

- **Keep the full-run gate conservative.** An unresolved diagnostic or model issue should direct work toward a bounded reproduction or inspection. It should not instruct an optimiser to change physics until a pass appears.
- **Treat stop snaps over 25 m and a sampled route deviation bound over 50 m as project review screens.** They are not thresholds established by the cited transport guidance. A flag needs a traceable disposition: source coordinate offset, wrong lane/side, genuine route deviation, or unresolved. A distance below a threshold is not a surveyed street match.
- **Separate model correction, uncertain assumption and intervention.** Moving a falsely matched stop to its actual road is a correction. Changing an unobserved driver parameter is sensitivity analysis. Building a physical boarding platform or a queue bypass is an intervention. Each needs its own source identity; comparisons must share the accepted baseline.
- **Add evidence-bound review dispositions when needed.** A reviewer should be able to explain why a screen was accepted, corrected or remains unresolved, citing the affected stop/edge and source. A generic `force` flag or silent reduction of the scheduled service would defeat the gate.
- **Do not call the empty case a certified physical lower bound.** It still contains imported geometry, junction rules, acceleration, speed assumptions and SUMO stop semantics. Even a zero-duration stop decelerates to zero, while `until` and `duration` interact; `parking=true` removes the stopped vehicle from traffic and requires a real-space feasibility review before being proposed. [SUMO stop definitions](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.html#stops-and-waypoints).

Public-data-only work can produce an auditable sensitivity study. It cannot claim calibrated current traffic without observations. FHWA calls for recent, time-varying, contemporaneous measurements, including both a local bottleneck and a system measure, and distinguishes device locations from actual control data. [FHWA data guidance](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop18036/chapter2.htm).

## Interventions worth testing once the relevant failure is understood

**Conditional signal priority with schedule-aware holding is the first controller comparison.** Request priority when a bus needs it; avoid accelerating an already early bus into more holding. Preserve clearance, pedestrian service and bounded requests, account for opposing requests, and record denied requests and cross-street effects. Start with the simpler published control logic before a learning agent. Its usefulness still depends on the bus being able to reach the stop line. These are adaptations of the control evidence above, not a predicted benefit for 701.

**Stop and boarding operations deserve equal attention.** All-door boarding and changes to fare collection can reduce dwell, but they require an actual operating change, equipment and accessibility review. In-lane boarding bulbs can avoid re-entry delay; a pull-out bay can instead provide space for holding but add merging delay. Compare feasible layouts at diagnosed locations rather than assuming every stop has an ideal bay. Do not remove stops merely to reduce runtime without including the extra walk and accessibility impacts. [NACTO's boarding guidance](https://nacto.org/publication/better-boarding-better-buses/), [NACTO boarding bulbs](https://nacto.org/publication/transit-street-design-guide/stations-stops/stop-configurations/boarding-bulb-stop/).

**Add vehicle circulation before claiming to prevent cascading late starts.** The present native model independently inserts each scheduled bus. A late inbound arrival therefore cannot delay the same vehicle's next outbound run as it would in a real block. Test explicit turnaround/recovery constraints, finite spare buses and driver resources once a defensible block scenario exists. Label inferred blocks as assumptions. SUMO supports repeating vehicle routes specifically so delay can carry into the next cycle; the tutorial contrasts them with independent vehicles that disappear after one run. [SUMO public transport tutorial](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Tutorials/PublicTransport.html).

**Maintain schedule adherence and headway diagnostics together.** Headway control is appropriate for frequent service, but can deliberately hold a bus away from its individual timetable. For this project it should initially be a separate sensitivity/control comparison, with exact timetable acceptance still visible. The published headway-control comparison finds a trade-off between regularity and holding and sensitivity to prediction quality. [Berrebi et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X17302000).

## State of the art: useful later, with conventional baselines

Original 2025 research combines multi-agent signal control with headway objectives, action constraints, yellow/all-red transitions and general-traffic rewards; its reported evidence is simulation-based. A newer bus-holding study, revised in October 2026, models a bidirectional timetable and fluctuating demand. Neither demonstrates that such a controller would solve this Porto model or eliminate field lateness. Their useful lesson for us is to represent passenger and traffic effects and constrain actions explicitly. [Long and Chung, 2025](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/10397/116430), [Zhang et al., version 3, 17 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20784v3).

A 2026 SUMO conference preprint presents `sumoITScontrol`, a Python/TraCI controller collection, and emphasises reproducible baselines, replicated trials and variance-aware reporting. It is a candidate for later benchmarking, not a reason to replace the current engine or trust an uncalibrated network. Any imported controller needs our same pedestrian, demand and timetable acceptance checks. [Riehl et al., 2026 preprint](https://sumo.dlr.de/preprints/pre-print-3284.pdf).

For now, adding RL training or calling the scene a digital twin would add complexity before resolving input and observation gaps. **Recommendation:** first establish verified fixed/generated control, conditional priority and holding baselines; only then compare predictive optimisation or constrained RL using the same frozen test cases and held-out scenarios.

## A small, code-first next-experiment plan

| Order | Experiment or review | Decision it must enable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep the matching short control; inspect its speed/stop/connector trace and decompose the 480-second Codiceira–Ermesinde budget. Review source timing semantics and the flagged stop matches. | Explain the residual five seconds under the zero-dawdling sensitivity without assuming the cause. Record any corrected model separately. |
| 2 | Inspect the first queue leaders in the 8-unfinished baseline and 42-unfinished package. Capture onset, intended connector, foes, signal state, stop/re-entry and downstream space in the next bounded case. | Distinguish a coding/controller deadlock, stop operation or capacity issue before proposing a remedy. A final-state leader alone does not identify the initiating cause. |
| 3 | Test one diagnosed remedy against the same accepted inputs in a bounded window. If it concerns signals, compare no new priority, existing priority and conditional priority; if it concerns queue access, test a specific short bypass/keep-clear design instead. | Falsify or support one explicit hypothesis. Keep all physical requests, warmup and boundary context; a cut-down network needs its own boundary validation. |
| 4 | After model issues have accepted dispositions, run a paired full-service baseline/candidate on a discovery seed. | Reject candidates that lose service, strand more passengers or transfer unacceptable delay/access loss to others. Preserve all 127 workings and 714 checks. |
| 5 | For promising candidates, use an initial four-seed variance estimate per condition and determine further replications; include uncertain demand/dwell/controller scenarios and fresh verification seeds. | Establish the declared simulation-compliance and robustness results separately from field claims. Full-service verification follows local discovery. |
| 6 | Remove or shorten one intervention at a time and rerun the affected discovery cases, followed by the complete acceptance matrix. | Find a smaller successful tested package. This is necessity testing in a finite catalog, not proof of the global minimum. |

Represent an experiment as `{accepted_model, frozen_demand, hypothesis, one_change_or_declared_interaction, paired_control, capture, budget, acceptance}`. Store results separately from presentation. Rank only feasible packages, then compare lane metres, junction/controller changes, stop works, operating resources, implementation phases and cost ranges. The technical and citizen reports should be generated from the same evaluated evidence. These are project architecture recommendations inferred from the research and the current failure pattern.

For the longer-term mode-shift story, keep travellers' origins, destinations and trip purposes while explicitly changing feasible mode choice, access time, passenger demand and capacity. Do not simply delete a percentage of car trips. SUMO can route intermodal person trips, but its documented routing cost does not supply a calibrated behavioural adoption forecast. MATSim's activity/replanning model is a possible later regional layer, coupled to local SUMO validation. Immediate disruption and longer-term adoption should remain separate scenarios with stated assumptions. [SUMO intermodal routing](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/IntermodalRouting.html), [MATSim user guide](https://www.matsim.org/files/book/partOne-latest.pdf).

## Practical conclusion

The most valuable change now is the investigation loop: **verified model → first-failure explanation → targeted operating/street remedy → complete service and passenger checks → replicated robustness → smallest successful tested package**. The current audit, trace capture, short reproduction and saved-state inspection implement the first part of that loop. The next substantive transport experiment should be selected by the trace, rather than another broad package of lanes, boarding and signals changed together.
